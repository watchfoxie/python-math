Set Algebra with Three Sets

Read three sets of words, A, B and C, given as lines of whitespace separated tokens, and report a few standard set quantities, ending with a check of the inclusion-exclusion principle for cardinalities: |A u B u C| = |A| + |B| + |C| - |A n B| - |A n C| - |B n C| + |A n B n C|

A word is either in a set or not, so repeated tokens on a line count once. The comparison of the two sides is part of the output: the program must say whether they agree.

Input:
Three lines: line 1 holds the tokens of A, line 2 those of B, line 3 those of C. A line may be empty, meaning the corresponding set is empty.

Output:
Five lines, in this order:
"union k e1 e2 ..." the union of the three sets, elements sorted lexicographically (ASCII order), the count first;
"intersection k e1 ..." the set A n B n C, same conventions;
"inclusion-exclusion v" the right hand side of the identity above;
"direct v" the cardinality |A u B u C| counted directly;
"agree" or "disagree" whether the two previous lines match.

Examples

Example 1:
input:
red green blue
green blue yellow
blue white

output:
union 5 blue green red white yellow
intersection 1 blue
inclusion-exclusion 5
direct 5
agree

Example 2:
input:
apple
<empty line>
apple pear

output:
union 2 apple pear
intersection 0
inclusion-exclusion 2
direct 2
agree
Here B is the empty set: the second input line is blank, and the third line holds the tokens of C.
